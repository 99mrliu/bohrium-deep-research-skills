# Interface Engineering of Li-ion Battery Cathodes via Al2O3 Coatings

## Introduction

Li-ion batteries (LIBs) are widely used in portable electronics and electric vehicles. The capacity fading of Li-ion cells at high voltage remains a major obstacle. Electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) has become a standard tool for probing interfacial resistance in Li-ion cells. The solid electrolyte interphase (SEI) formed on the anode strongly affects cycling stability.

## Methods

Li-ion coin cells were assembled with LiNi0.8Co0.1Mn0.1O2 (NCM811) cathodes. Al2O3 coatings of 2 nm were deposited by atomic layer deposition (ALD). Cells were cycled at 1C between 2.8 V and 4.4 V. EIS data were collected from 100 kHz to 10 mHz with a perturbation amplitude of 5 mV.

## Results

EIS measurements showed that the interfacial resistance of coated cells decreased by 32% after 200 cycles. The solid electrolyte interphase on coated anodes was thinner and more uniform. ## Conclusion

Al2O3 coatings stabilize the Li-ion cathode interface and suppress SEI growth. These findings demonstrate a scalable route to durable high-voltage Li-ion batteries.
