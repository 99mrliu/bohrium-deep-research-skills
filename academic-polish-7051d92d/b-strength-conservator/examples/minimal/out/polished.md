Background: Inflammation is thought to contribute to the progression of metabolic liver disease, but the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. In this study, we investigated the role of the NLRP3 inflammasome in a mouse model of steatohepatitis. Methods: Primary hepatocytes were isolated from wild-type and NLRP3-knockout mice. Cells were exposed to palmitic acid for 24 hours, and inflammasome activation was measured by immunoblotting.

Results: Palmitic acid increased caspase-1 cleavage in wild-type cells, whereas this effect was largely absent in knockout cells. In vivo, NLRP3-deficient mice showed reduced liver fibrosis after eight weeks of high-fat feeding. The treatment may improve insulin sensitivity in some animals, although the effect did not reach statistical significance. Biomarker Y is associated with disease severity in this cohort. There are several pieces of evidence that oxidative stress amplifies inflammasome signaling in vitro. Our findings demonstrate that NLRP3 contributes to liver injury under these conditions. This work could open new directions for the design of future therapies. Conclusion: Further studies are needed to confirm these observations in human samples.
