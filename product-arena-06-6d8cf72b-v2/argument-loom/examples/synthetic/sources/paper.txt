Lumen-Coated Beads Moderate Peak Temperature in a Bench Soil Assay

Abstract
We tested whether lumen-coated glass beads reduce heat retention in a bench soil assay. In three paired trials, coated beads reached a mean peak of 18.0 °C, compared with 20.0 °C for uncoated beads. The finding motivates larger tests rather than immediate deployment.

Introduction
Passive cooling media can buffer temperature swings, but their behavior in shallow soil beds is uncertain. We therefore asked whether a lumen coating changes peak temperature under a controlled heat pulse.

Methods
Three paired trials used identical 200 g soil beds, one coated and one uncoated 40 g bead layer, and the same 10-minute heat pulse. Bed positions were randomized for each pair, and temperatures were recorded simultaneously by shielded probes at a depth of 2 cm. The prespecified endpoint was the within-pair difference in peak temperature.

Results
Across the three pairs, mean peak temperatures were 18.0 °C for coated beads and 20.0 °C for uncoated beads. Individual within-pair reductions were 1.8 °C, 2.1 °C, and 2.1 °C; no adverse physical change was observed.

Discussion
The paired design and simultaneous measurements support a within-assay interpretation of the temperature difference. However, only three pairs were tested in one chamber with one soil mixture, so the estimate may not generalize to field moisture, airflow, or longer heating cycles.

Conclusion
Under this specific bench protocol, lumen coating was associated with a 2.0 °C lower mean peak temperature. Larger multi-chamber and outdoor studies are needed before practical cooling claims can be made.
