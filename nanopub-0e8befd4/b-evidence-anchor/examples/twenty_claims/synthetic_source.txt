GreenTune: a feedback irrigation controller for greenhouse tomato
H. Sample, I. Placeholder. Synthetic Horticulture Letters, 2023. (fictional)

Abstract
GreenTune is a feedback irrigation controller that adjusts daily irrigation
volumes from soil moisture sensors. In a 12-week trial across 24 greenhouses,
GreenTune reduced water use by 18.5% relative to timer-based control while
maintaining marketable yield (9.2 kg/m2 vs 9.1 kg/m2). The controller lowers
irrigation frequency when the measured transpira-
tion rate falls below a calibrated threshold. We observed no significant
difference in fruit soluble solids between the two schedules.
Methods (excerpt)
Soil volumetric water content was logged every 10 minutes at 15 cm depth.
Irrigation was triggered when the running average dropped below 35% VWC for
more than 30 minutes. The calibrated transpiration threshold was set during a
7-day baseline period. Daily irrigation totals were summed at midnight and
exported per zone; daily irrigation records were audited weekly by staff.
The trial ran from March to June 2022 in two climate zones. Each greenhouse
was split into a GreenTune zone and a timer zone of equal area.
Results (excerpt)
Across the 24 greenhouses, mean daily water use was 2.4 L/m2 under GreenTune
and 2.9 L/m2 under timer control. Fruit mass per plant was 3.8 kg with
GreenTune and 3.7 kg with the timer schedule. Sensor faults occurred in 3 of
288 sensor-weeks and were excluded from the analysis.

Limitations (excerpt)
GreenTune is not evaluated for open-field crops. The controller requires a
mains power supply; a battery variant is planned but untested. Calibration
currently assumes loam soils and may drift in sandy substrates.
