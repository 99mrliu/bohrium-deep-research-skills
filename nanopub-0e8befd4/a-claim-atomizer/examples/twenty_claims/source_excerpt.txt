AlignX: a two-pass short-read aligner for noisy long k-mers
F. Example, G. Demo. Synthetic Journal of Methods, 2024. doi:10.0000/synth.2024.001 (fictional)

[Page 1, Abstract]
AlignX is a two-pass short-read aligner that extends the seed-and-extend scheme of
ClassicAlign. It automatically chooses between ungapped and gapped extension and
supports both single-end and paired-end reads. On the simulated S1 dataset, AlignX
achieved 97.4% mapping accuracy with default parameters, outperforming ClassicAlign
(94.1%) and FastMap (95.8%). We propose that the second pass may reduce soft-clipping
artifacts in repetitive regions, though this remains to be tested on real data.

[Page 2, Methods, excerpt]
The first pass uses a hash index with k=19 and a maximum of 3 candidate bins per read.
The second pass re-scores only reads whose first-pass mapping quality is below 30.
AlignX requires at least 8 GB of memory for human-genome indexing and runs in time
linear in the number of reads.

[Page 2, Results, excerpt]
On the real R1 dataset (Illumina-like, 100 bp), AlignX mapped 96.2% of reads, compared
with 95.0% for ClassicAlign and 94.4% for FastMap. Runtime on R1 was 41 minutes for
AlignX, about 1.3x slower than ClassicAlign but 2.1x faster than FastMap. On reads
shorter than 60 bp, AlignX showed no significant accuracy advantage over ClassicAlign.

[Page 3, Limitations, excerpt]
AlignX currently does not support reads longer than 300 bp. The hash index must be
rebuilt when k changes, which can take up to 2 hours for the human genome.
