Ion-Conductive Hydrogel Electrolytes for Flexible Zinc Batteries: A Synthetic Demonstration Study

Wei Lin, Sara Okonkwo, and Tomás Herrera
Journal of Synthetic Materials Letters, 2024
DOI: 10.52810/jsml.2024.0417

Abstract

Flexible zinc-ion batteries require electrolytes that combine high ionic conductivity with mechanical resilience. In this synthetic demonstration study we report a polyacrylamide-alginate double-network hydrogel, designated AlphaGel-7, that reaches an ionic conductivity of 41 mS/cm at room temperature while retaining 92 percent of its initial capacity after 1200 bending cycles. 1. Introduction

Aqueous zinc-ion batteries are attractive for wearable electronics because they are intrinsically non-flammable and use abundant materials. However, liquid electrolytes leak under deformation, and conventional gel electrolytes sacrifice conductivity for mechanical strength. The central trade-off between ionic transport and toughness motivates the double-network design explored here.

2. Methods

AlphaGel-7 was synthesized by free-radical polymerization of acrylamide within a calcium-crosslinked alginate scaffold, followed by soaking in 2 M zinc sulfate. Ionic conductivity was measured by electrochemical impedance spectroscopy across a frequency range of 1 Hz to 1 MHz. Bending fatigue was evaluated at a radius of 5 mm for 1200 cycles. All procedures described here are fictional and no real experiments were performed.

3. Results

The hydrogel achieved an ionic conductivity of 41 mS/cm at 25 degrees Celsius, roughly three times that of the single-network control. Symmetric zinc cells with AlphaGel-7 cycled for 800 hours at 1 mA/cm2 without short circuit. Capacity retention after 1200 bending cycles was 92 percent, compared with 61 percent for the control gel. These numbers are synthetic example values.

4. Discussion

The double-network architecture dissipates mechanical stress through reversible ionic crosslinks while preserving continuous water-rich conduction pathways. We caution that the synthetic values in this demonstration are not comparable to real literature and exist only to exercise verbatim quote verification.

5. Conclusion

AlphaGel-7 illustrates how a double-network hydrogel can in principle reconcile ionic conductivity with bending endurance, although the present study is entirely synthetic and draws no real scientific conclusion.
