BetaMesh Covalent Organic Framework Catalysts for Photocatalytic Hydrogen Evolution: A Synthetic Demonstration Report

Aiko Tanaka, Priya Raman, and Jonas Weber
Synthetic Catalysis Communications, 2023
DOI: 10.52810/scc.2023.0231

Abstract

Covalent organic frameworks offer tunable platforms for photocatalytic hydrogen evolution. In this synthetic demonstration report we describe BetaMesh-3, a fictional beta-ketoenamine-linked framework that exhibits an apparent hydrogen evolution rate of 12.4 mmol/g/h under visible light in the presence of a sacrificial donor. This document is entirely synthetic and serves only to demonstrate quote verification and reference identity checking.

1. Introduction

Photocatalytic water splitting remains limited by charge recombination and photocorrosion. Framework catalysts with ordered channels could in principle separate carriers spatially, but reported activities vary widely with donor chemistry and illumination conditions, making cross-study comparison difficult.

2. Synthesis and Characterization

BetaMesh-3 was notionally prepared by solvothermal condensation of trialdehyde and diamine monomers. The synthetic record claims a BET surface area of 1480 m2/g and an optical band gap of 2.1 eV. No real synthesis took place; all characterization values are fabricated for demonstration.

3. Photocatalytic Testing

Under visible irradiation with ascorbic acid as sacrificial donor, BetaMesh-3 showed a hydrogen evolution rate of 12.4 mmol/g/h. Without any donor the rate fell below 0.2 mmol/g/h, illustrating the sensitivity of the measurement to reaction conditions. After five consecutive runs the activity decreased by 18 percent, which the fictional authors attribute to partial pore blocking.

4. Discussion

The donor-dependent activity underlines a recurring problem in photocatalysis literature: headline rates are frequently reported under optimized sacrificial conditions and are not directly comparable across studies. Any honest synthesis of such evidence must record the exact conditions alongside the quoted numbers.

5. Conclusion

BetaMesh-3 is a synthetic construct; its demonstration values illustrate how condition-sensitive photocatalytic metrics can be, and no real catalytic claim is made.
