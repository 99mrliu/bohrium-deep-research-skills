Short Communication: Sleep, Caffeine and Memory (Synthetic Sample)

Girardeau and colleagues showed that suppressing hippocampal sharp-wave
ripples during sleep impairs spatial memory in rats [1]. This proves that
sleep consolidates declarative memory in humans [1]. In a large
prospective cohort, higher caffeine intake was associated with slower
cognitive decline [2]. Therefore, daily caffeine prevents Alzheimer
disease [2]. The molecular mechanisms of sleep-dependent consolidation
have been reviewed comprehensively [3]. A further mechanism involving
astrocyte signaling was demonstrated recently [9].
