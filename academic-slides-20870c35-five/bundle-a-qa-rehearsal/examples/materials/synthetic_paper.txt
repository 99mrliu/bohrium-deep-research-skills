CapFlow-3: A Low-Cost Differential-Pressure Capillary Flow Sensor
（合成论文，任何机构、数据与数字均为虚构）

Abstract
--------
We present CapFlow-3, a low-cost capillary flow sensor built around a
differential-pressure readout. Across n=48 bench trials the device achieves
12.7% relative error against the reference rig. Preliminary soak tests suggest
the readout remains stable over 72 h of continuous operation.

1. Introduction
---------------
Affordable flow metering in the microlitre regime remains a bottleneck for
teaching laboratories and field deployments. Commercial units are accurate
but expensive. (合成正文，从略。)

2. Methods
----------
2.1 Sensing principle. The differential-pressure readout linearizes the
capillary response via a two-point calibration: a baseline point is fixed at
0.42 mL/min, and the slope is re-checked at the operating point. The
calibration curve and residuals are shown in Figure 1.

2.2 Bench protocol. We ran n=48 parallel trials against the reference rig at
a controlled temperature of 25 °C with aqueous samples only.

3. Results
----------
Across the n=48 trials the relative error against the reference bench is
12.7% (see Table 2 for the per-trial data). Unit cost is 38 USD per device,
compared with 210 USD for the commercial unit; with self-calibration disabled
on both sides, the measured noise floors match within 0.3 mV.

Preliminary tests with three devices suggest the readout holds its
calibration over 72 h of continuous operation; no humidity cycling has been
performed yet.

4. Limitations
--------------
Findings are limited to aqueous samples at 25 °C. Organic solvents and
temperature cycling are untested. The 72 h stability result is preliminary
and based on only three devices.
