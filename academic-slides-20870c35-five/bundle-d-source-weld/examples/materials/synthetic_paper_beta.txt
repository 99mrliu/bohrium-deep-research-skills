Lucia Moretti and Kwame Asante

Abstract

Recycled carbon fiber is often dismissed for load-bearing parts. In this synthetic demonstration study we show that careful realignment of reclaimed fibers yields laminates that, in our fictional tests, the recycled laminates reached a mean tensile strength of 612 MPa, about 78 percent of a virgin-fiber reference laminate. All contents of this document are synthetic and created solely to demonstrate the source-weld-verifier skill pipeline.

1. Introduction

Aerospace and wind-energy sectors generate large volumes of uncured prepreg offcuts and end-of-life composite waste. Reclaiming the fiber while preserving mechanical performance is an open problem, and this synthetic study reports one fictional alignment route.

2. Methods

Reclaimed fibers were realigned by a fictional hydrodynamic process and consolidated into eight-ply laminates by compression molding at 180 degrees Celsius for 90 minutes. Tensile tests followed a synthetic in-house protocol. All procedures described here are fictional and no real experiments were performed.

3. Results

The realigned laminates reached a mean tensile strength of 612 MPa with a modulus of 58 GPa. Despite the alignment step, porosity remained below 12 percent in all recycled panels, compared with 3 percent in the virgin reference. Fractography indicated fiber pull-out as the dominant failure mode.
