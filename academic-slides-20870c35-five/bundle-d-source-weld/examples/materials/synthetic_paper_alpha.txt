Damp-Heat Stable Perovskite Modules via Interfacial Welding: A Synthetic Demonstration Study

Aiko Tanabe, Priya Raman, and Jonas Weber
Journal of Synthetic Photovoltaics Letters, 2024

Abstract

Perovskite solar modules degrade rapidly under damp-heat stress. In this synthetic demonstration study we report an interfacial welding layer, designated WeldLayer-3, that improves module durability: encapsulated minimodules reached a power conversion efficiency of 23.4 percent and retained 91 percent of its initial efficiency after 1000 hours of damp-heat exposure at 85 degrees Celsius and 85 percent relative humidity. All contents of this document are synthetic and created solely to demonstrate the source-weld-verifier skill pipeline.

1. Introduction

Perovskite photovoltaics combine high efficiency with low-cost processing, but their sensitivity to heat and moisture blocks deployment. Interface engineering is widely regarded as the key lever, and this synthetic study explores one fictional welding approach.

2. Methods

WeldLayer-3 was deposited by blade coating from a 40 milligram per milliliter precursor solution and annealed for 5 minutes at 100 degrees Celsius. Minimodules with an active area of 25 cm2 were encapsulated and aged in a damp-heat chamber. All procedures described here are fictional and no real experiments were performed.

3. Results

Spectroscopic ellipsometry showed that a bandgap of 1.68 eV was measured for the triple-cation film after welding, unchanged from the unwelded control. The champion minimodule reached a power conversion efficiency of 23.4 percent under one sun. After 1000 hours of damp-heat exposure at 85 degrees Celsius, the welded modules retained 91 percent of their initial efficiency, whereas unwelded controls retained only 54 percent.
