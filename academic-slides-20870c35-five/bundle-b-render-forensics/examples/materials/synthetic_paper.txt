Title: Drift Compensation for a Low-Cost Humidity Sensor Array
Authors: A. Researcher, B. Collaborator (synthetic personas)

Abstract
Low-cost polymer-film humidity sensors drift over multi-week deployments.
We built a 24-channel array and a two-point recalibration scheme that
estimates per-channel baseline drift every 6 hours against a single
reference cell. In a 21-day continuous run with 3 replicate arrays,
median absolute error dropped from 1.84 %RH (raw) to 0.77 %RH
(compensated), a 58% reduction.

1. Introduction
Humidity monitoring in storage and greenhouse settings needs many cheap
sensors, but polymer-film elements drift. Central question: can periodic
reference-based recalibration keep a cheap array within 1 %RH over three
weeks without per-channel lab calibration?

2. Methods
2.1 Hardware. 24 polymer-film channels multiplexed to one ADC; one
sealed reference cell held at fixed humidity.
2.2 Drift model. Per-channel baseline estimated every 6 hours as the
median residual against the reference; two-point recalibration applied
between estimates.
2.3 Evaluation. 21-day continuous run, 3 replicate arrays, ambient
temperature cycling 18-31 C.

3. Results
Figure 1 (exhibit: error-over-time curve). Median absolute error fell
from 1.84 %RH (raw) to 0.77 %RH (compensated); 95th percentile fell
from 3.9 to 1.6 %RH. Residual drift correlates with temperature swings
(Pearson r = 0.61).

4. Limitations
Single site, single season, 21 days only; the reference cell itself
ages; results are preliminary and may not transfer to condensing
environments.
