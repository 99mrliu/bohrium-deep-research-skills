# Photocatalytic Degradation of Rhodamine B over g-C3N4 under Visible Light

Synthetic Example University, Department of Environmental Materials. This document is a synthetic example for skill demonstration only and does not describe real experiments.

We synthesized graphitic carbon nitride (g-C3N4) by thermal polycondensation of melamine at 550 C for 4 h in a muffle furnace.

The photocatalytic experiments used Rhodamine B (RhB) as the target pollutant with an initial concentration of 10 mg/L, and the catalyst dosage was 0.5 g/L in a 100 mL aqueous suspension.

A 300 W xenon lamp with a 420 nm cut-off filter provided visible light irradiation, and the suspension was stirred in the dark for 30 min to reach adsorption-desorption equilibrium before illumination.

Under visible light irradiation, the g-C3N4 catalyst achieved 96% removal of RhB within 90 min, while the blank control without catalyst showed less than 5% removal.

The degradation kinetics followed pseudo-first-order behavior with an apparent rate constant of 0.035 min^-1 for g-C3N4, about 3.1 times that of bulk reference samples.

The BET specific surface area of the g-C3N4 sample was 52 m2/g, and the material remained stable over four recycling runs with less than 8% activity loss.
