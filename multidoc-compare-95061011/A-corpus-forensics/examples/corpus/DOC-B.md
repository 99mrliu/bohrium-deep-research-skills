# Visible-Light-Driven N-Doped TiO2 for Methylene Blue Decolorization

Institute of Synthetic Photochemistry, Sample City. This document is a synthetic example for skill demonstration only and does not describe real experiments.

Nitrogen-doped TiO2 (N-TiO2) was prepared by a sol-gel method using titanium isopropoxide and urea as the nitrogen source, followed by calcination at 450 C for 2 h.

The decolorization tests employed methylene blue (MB) as the model dye with an initial concentration of 20 mg/L, and the catalyst dosage was 1.0 g/L in a 250 mL aqueous suspension.

A 300 W xenon lamp equipped with a 420 nm cut-off filter supplied visible light, and the mixture was magnetically stirred throughout the experiment.

The N-TiO2 sample achieved 88% removal of methylene blue within 120 min under visible light, whereas undoped TiO2 removed only 31% under identical conditions.

The apparent pseudo-first-order rate constant for MB decolorization over N-TiO2 was 0.017 min^-1, roughly twice that of the undoped reference.

No BET surface area measurement was performed in this synthetic study, and recycling stability was not evaluated.
