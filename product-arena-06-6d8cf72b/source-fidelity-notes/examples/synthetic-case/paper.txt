Aerolite-7: a low-cost ceramic thermal barrier coating for small turbines
J. Example and R. Placeholder, Institute of Synthetic Studies

Abstract
We present Aerolite-7, a plasma-sprayed ceramic coating intended for
small turbine blades. In furnace-cycle tests at 1100 C the coating
survived 420 cycles before spallation, compared with 265 cycles for a
conventional YSZ baseline. The porosity of Aerolite-7 was 14 percent.

1. Introduction
Thermal barrier coatings extend the service life of turbine blades by
reducing metal surface temperature. Conventional yttria-stabilized
zirconia (YSZ) coatings are costly to deposit at small scale. Aerolite-7
replaces part of the YSZ feedstock with a synthetic mullite filler.

2. Methods
Coatings of 300 micrometer thickness were deposited by atmospheric
plasma spraying onto nickel alloy coupons. Furnace-cycle tests heated
samples to 1100 C in 20 minutes, held for 40 minutes, and forced-air
cooled. Porosity was measured by image analysis on polished sections.

3. Results
Aerolite-7 survived a mean of 420 cycles (n = 6, sd = 31) before
spallation, while the YSZ baseline survived 265 cycles (n = 6, sd = 24).
Porosity was 14 percent for Aerolite-7 and 11 percent for YSZ. Bond
strength measured by pull-off testing was 32 MPa for Aerolite-7.

4. Discussion
The longer cyclic life is consistent with strain tolerance from the
higher porosity. The improvement was observed only at 1100 C; at 900 C
the difference was not significant. We did not test erosion resistance,
and the synthetic mullite supply was laboratory grade.

5. Conclusion
Aerolite-7 extends furnace-cycle life at 1100 C relative to a YSZ
baseline under laboratory conditions; field validation remains open.
