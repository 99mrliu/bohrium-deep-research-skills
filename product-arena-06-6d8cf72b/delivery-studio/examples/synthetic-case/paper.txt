Low-cost soil moisture sensor network for smallholder plots
M. Dummy and P. Example, Institute of Fabricated Agronomy

Abstract
We deployed a 12-node capacitive soil moisture network on three
smallholder plots for one growing season. Nodes sampled every 15 minutes
and achieved 96 percent packet delivery over a LoRa link. The sensor
drift after one season was 2.1 percent of reading.

1. Introduction
Irrigation advice for smallholder plots is limited by the cost of soil
moisture sensing. We built a capacitive sensor node at 14 USD per unit
and tested it under field conditions.

2. Deployment
Twelve nodes were installed at 10 cm and 30 cm depths on three plots.
Data were transmitted over LoRa to a single gateway. The season lasted
16 weeks.

3. Findings
Packet delivery averaged 96 percent over the season. Sensor drift after
16 weeks was 2.1 percent of reading against gravimetric references.
Irrigation events were detected within one sampling interval in 41 of 44
cases.

4. Limitations
One season and three plots limit generality. Saline soils were not
tested. The 14 USD cost excludes the gateway.

5. Conclusion
The low-cost network delivered reliable seasonal monitoring on three
plots; multi-season durability remains open.
