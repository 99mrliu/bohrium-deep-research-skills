The Counting Bloom Sketch: space-efficient approximate membership with deletion
S. Toy and A. Mock, Department of Fabricated Computing

Abstract
We introduce the Counting Bloom Sketch (CBS), a probabilistic set
representation that supports element deletion. On synthetic workloads
with one million keys, CBS uses 9.6 bits per key at a false positive
rate of 0.9 percent, while a plain Bloom filter cannot delete at all.

1. Introduction
Approximate membership structures trade a small error rate for large
memory savings. The classic Bloom filter answers membership queries but
does not support deletion. The Counting Bloom Sketch replaces each bit
with a small counter.

2. Design
CBS allocates m counters of 4 bits each. Insertion increments k hash
positions; deletion decrements them. A counter saturates at 15, so
overflow is possible when many collisions occur on one position.

3. Evaluation
We generated one million synthetic keys with a uniform random source.
At 9.6 bits per key the observed false positive rate was 0.9 percent.
Deletion of 10 percent of keys kept the false positive rate below
1.1 percent. Throughput reached 1.4 million inserts per second.

4. Limitations
The evaluation uses uniform synthetic keys only; skewed real workloads
were not measured. Counter overflow was not observed at our scale but
remains possible for adversarial inputs.

5. Conclusion
CBS supports deletion with 4-bit counters at modest memory cost on
uniform synthetic workloads.
