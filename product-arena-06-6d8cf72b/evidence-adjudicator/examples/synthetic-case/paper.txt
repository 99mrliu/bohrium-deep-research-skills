Micro-inverter versus string inverter yield on shaded urban rooftops
K. Sample and L. Dummy, Institute of Invented Energy Systems

Abstract
We compared micro-inverter and string-inverter rooftop photovoltaic
systems on 24 simulated urban houses over one synthetic year. Under
partial shading the micro-inverter configuration produced 8.2 percent
more annual energy per installed kilowatt-peak.

1. Introduction
Urban rooftops suffer partial shading from chimneys and trees. String
inverters lose disproportionate energy when one panel is shaded. We
tested whether module-level power electronics recover this loss in a
simulated housing stock.

2. Setup
Twenty-four synthetic roof geometries were generated procedurally. Each
roof was simulated twice: once with a string inverter and once with
micro-inverters, using the same irradiance series. Shading objects were
assigned from a fixed random seed.

3. Results
Across the 24 roofs the micro-inverter configuration yielded a mean of
8.2 percent more annual energy per kWp (range 3.1 to 14.7 percent). On
unshaded reference roofs the difference was 0.4 percent, within
simulation tolerance. Inverter cost was not modelled.

4. Discussion
The gain concentrates on roofs with more than two hours of daily shade.
The study did not measure real hardware; all results are simulation
output with fixed seed 42.

5. Conclusion
Module-level power electronics recover most shading loss in the
simulated housing stock; economic comparison remains out of scope.
