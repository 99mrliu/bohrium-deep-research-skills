Remission Induction by Compound ZX-101 in Moderate Atopic Dermatitis:
A Single-Center Open-Label Study

Wei Chen, Lijuan Zhao, and Maria Gomez
Department of Dermatology, Example University Hospital, Example City

Abstract
Background: Atopic dermatitis (AD) is a chronic inflammatory skin disease
with limited long-term treatment options. Compound ZX-101 is a novel oral
JAK inhibitor candidate. Methods: We conducted a single-center, open-label
study of ZX-101 over 12 weeks in adults with moderate AD. The primary
endpoint was the proportion of patients achieving EASI-75 at week 12.
Results: ZX-101 produced rapid and sustained improvement in disease
severity scores. Conclusions: ZX-101 is a promising therapy for moderate
AD and warrants further evaluation in randomized trials.

Keywords: atopic dermatitis; JAK inhibitor; open-label study; EASI-75

1. Introduction
Atopic dermatitis affects up to 10 percent of adults worldwide and imposes
a substantial burden on quality of life. Topical corticosteroids remain
the mainstay of therapy, but many patients with moderate disease
experience inadequate control or adverse effects with long-term use. Oral
Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors have emerged as a new therapeutic class, yet
real-world evidence for newer candidates such as ZX-101 remains scarce.
The present study aimed to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of ZX-101
in adults with moderate atopic dermatitis in a routine clinical setting.
2. Methods
2.1 Study design and participants
We conducted a single-center, open-label, prospective study at Example
University Hospital between March 2024 and June 2025. We enrolled 120
adult participants (aged 18 to 65 years) with a physician diagnosis of
moderate atopic dermatitis (EASI score 8 to 21). All participants
received ZX-101 15 mg once daily for 12 weeks. Concomitant topical
therapy was permitted if the regimen had been stable for at least 4
weeks before enrollment.

2.2 Outcomes
The primary endpoint was the proportion of participants achieving
EASI-75 (at least 75 percent improvement in Eczema Area and Severity
Index) at week 12. Secondary endpoints included change in pruritus
numeric rating scale (NRS) and the incidence of adverse events.

3. Results
3.1 Participant disposition
Of the screened patients, 120 participants were enrolled and received at
least one dose of ZX-101. The baseline characteristics of the analysis
population are summarized in Table 1.

3.2 Efficacy
At week 12, 58 of 102 participants (56.9 percent) achieved EASI-75. Mean
pruritus NRS decreased from 6.8 at baseline to 3.1 at week 12.
4. Discussion
In this open-label study, ZX-101 was associated with clinically
meaningful improvement in signs and symptoms of moderate atopic
dermatitis over 12 weeks. The observed EASI-75 response rate is within
the range reported for other oral JAK inhibitors, although cross-trial
comparisons should be made with caution. The single-center, open-label
design limits the strength of any causal interpretation.

5. Conclusion
Our findings demonstrate that ZX-101 definitively causes durable
remission of moderate atopic dermatitis in adult patients. Clinicians
should consider ZX-101 as a first-line oral therapy for this population.
