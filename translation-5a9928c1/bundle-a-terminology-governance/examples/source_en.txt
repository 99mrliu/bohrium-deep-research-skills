Solute segregation at grain boundaries in yttria-stabilized zirconia
<!-- zone:abstract -->
Grain boundary (GB) chemistry governs the ionic conductivity of yttria-stabilized zirconia (YSZ), the standard electrolyte of the solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC). We quantify gadolinium segregation at Σ3 GBs in SOFC-grade YSZ by atomistic simulation, and we find that segregation lowers the GB resistance by 40% at 800 °C. The result revises the long-standing assumption that GBs are always resistive blocking layers.
<!-- zone:body -->
Figure 1 shows the equilibrium solute profile across a Σ3 grain boundary in YSZ. For each GB, the excess volume at the boundary plane traps dopant cations, while the elastic strain field repels them; the competition sets the net segregation free energy. Table 1 lists the segregation energy for five GBs, ranging from −0.8 eV to −1.9 eV. A more negative segregation free energy implies a stronger driving force for dopant accumulation at the grain boundary.

<!-- zone:conclusion -->
In conclusion, GB segregation in YSZ can be beneficial for SOFC electrolytes: a 40% reduction of GB resistance at 800 °C is predicted for gadolinium-doped compositions. The findings suggest that grain boundary engineering should target segregating dopants rather than avoid them.
