Enhancing Power Conversion Efficiency and Open-Circuit Voltage of Perovskite Solar Cells via Charge Transport Layer Engineering

Abstract
Perovskite solar cell (PSC) devices have progressed rapidly in the past decade.
Power conversion efficiency (PCE) values for the best PSC devices now exceed 25%, while the open-circuit voltage continues to improve.

Introduction
The perovskite solar cell architecture typically sandwiches an absorber between two selective contacts.
The charge transport layer extracts carriers from the absorber and delivers them to the electrode.
A poorly designed charge transport layer causes interfacial recombination and lowers the open-circuit voltage.
The Charge Transport Layer engineering strategy reported here uses a self-assembled monolayer.
This monolayer raises the Open-Circuit Voltage by roughly 60 mV in every perovskite solar cell we tested.
We fabricated forty PSC devices under identical conditions.
The average power conversion efficiency rose from 21.3% to 24.1%.
The gain in PCE correlated strongly with the increase in open-circuit voltage.
Every device with an optimized charge transport layer retained 95% of its initial efficiency after 500 hours.
Control devices without the modified charge transport layer degraded twice as fast.

Conclusion
The optimized charge transport layer simultaneously improves the open-circuit voltage, the power conversion efficiency, and the stability of the perovskite solar cell.
