# Tidal Battery Aging in a Fictional Coastal Microgrid

## 1. Introduction

Coastal microgrids increasingly depend on tidal battery banks, and
recent field surveys [3] report accelerated aging under salt fog.
Independent laboratory work on electrode corrosion [1] points in the
same direction, while grid-scale modeling of whole storage fleets [7]
remains scarce and mostly theoretical. Operators therefore plan
maintenance cycles from rules of thumb rather than from evidence.
A widely circulated industry whitepaper even cites unpublished
workshop notes [9], which nobody has been able to retrieve.

Early pilot projects [4] were small and often poorly documented,
but their maintenance logs remain the richest public record of
long-term salt-fog exposure for battery containers.

The present study combines both traditions: we run a controlled
field campaign at a single site, but we interpret the results
against the published survey and laboratory baselines. Our goal is
a practical aging model that a microgrid operator can apply with
nothing more than a salinity logger and a maintenance ledger.

## 2. Methods

We instrumented three battery containers over eighteen months at the
fictional Port Meridian test site. Humidity and salinity were logged
every ten minutes, and the raw data streams were archived following
the consortium data-management plan [7,3]. Container firmware was
cross-checked against the calibration notes [6] before each campaign,
and any sensor drifting more than two percent was replaced. All three
containers were repainted midway through the study so that coating
effects could be separated from corrosion effects in the analysis.

## 3. Results

Capacity fade correlated strongly with measured salt deposition
across all three containers [3,7]. The correlation held after
controlling for repaint cycles and firmware versions. Containers
moored near the splash zone aged fastest.

We caution that the eighteen-month window is still short relative to
the advertised service life of tidal battery banks. The corrosion-only
baseline explains roughly half of the observed fade, which suggests
that mechanical stress from repeated crane lifts and thermal cycling
also contribute. A longer follow-up study, ideally covering two full
overhaul cycles, would be needed to separate these factors cleanly.

Future work will pair the salinity logger with a cheap strain gauge
on the container lifting points, so that mechanical stress can be
logged on the same ten-minute cadence as the environmental channels.
We also plan to publish the anonymized maintenance ledger together
with the aging model, because a model that operators cannot audit
against their own records is unlikely to be trusted in the field.

## Appendix A. Supplementary notes

The supplementary log format is described in the maintenance
manual [2]. Raw hourly aggregates are available from the authors
on request. The aggregation script groups records by container,
then by week, and discards any week with fewer than five valid
sampling days before computing summary statistics.
